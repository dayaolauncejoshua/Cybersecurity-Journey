**NIST Digital Forensics Process**

    NIST describes a general digital forensics process with 4 phases:
    Collection → Examination → Analysis → Reporting

    1. Collection - Gather the evidence
    Identify all devices and data that may contain evidence.

    Examples:
    Computers/laptops
    Phones
    USB drives
    Cameras
    Hard drives

    The important part is to preserve the original evidence and properly document what was collected. Gather evidence without altering it

    
    2. Examination - Filter the evidence
    There may be a huge amount of collected data, so investigators filter it to find information relevant to the case.

    Example:
    A camera contains 10,000 photos, but investigators only need photos taken between June 1–5.

    Extract the useful/relevant data from the large amount of evidence.


    3. Analysis - Figure out what happened
    Investigators analyze the relevant evidence and correlate information from different sources.

    Example:
    Laptop logs
        +
    Phone messages
        +
    Network activity
        ↓
    Timeline of events

    The goal is to determine what activities occurred, when they occurred, and how they relate to the investigation. Draw conclusions from the evidence and reconstruct events.


    4. Reporting - Document the findings
    The investigators create a detailed report containing:

    Investigation methodology
    Evidence examined
    Findings
    Conclusions
    Recommendations, when appropriate

    The report may be given to law enforcement, management, or other relevant parties.

    An executive summary is useful because not everyone reading the report will have technical knowledge.

    Clearly communicate what was investigated and what was discovered.


**Types of Digital Forensics**

    Different devices and environments require different forensic techniques.

    1. Computer Forensics - focuses on computers and laptops. 
    
    Example of evidence: Files, logs, browser history, deleted data.


    2. Mobile Forensics - focuses on Phones/tablets

    Example of evidence: Calls, messages, GPS, apps


    3. Network Forensics - focuses on network activity

    Examples: Network traffic, connection logs, IP addresses


    4. Database Forensics - Databases

    Examples: Modified/deleted data, unauthorized access, data exfiltration


    5. Cloud Forensics - Cloud environments

    Examples: Cloud logs, accounts, stored data, activity


    Email Forensics - Emails

    Examples: Phishing, fraud, attachments, headers

**Key take:**

    Digital forensics follows: Collection → Examination → Analysis → Reporting.
