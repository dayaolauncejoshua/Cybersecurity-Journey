**Digital Forensics**

    Forensics = using methods and procedures to investigate crimes and find evidence.

    Digital forensics is the branch of forensics focused on cybercrime and digital devices.

    is the process of collecting, preserving, and analyzing digital evidence to determine what happened during a security incident.

    It involves using specialized tools and techniques to examine devices and digital data, find evidence, and determine what happened.

    Example: Bank Robbery
    Law enforcement legally searches a suspect's home and finds:

    Laptop
    Mobile phone
    Hard drive
    USB device

    The devices are handed over to a digital forensics team.

    The team securely collects and examines the evidence in a forensic laboratory.


    Laptop - Digital map of the bank used for planning
    Hard drive - Bank entrance and escape routes
    Hard drive - Information about the bank's security controls and plans to bypass them
    Laptop - Photos/videos of previous robberies
    Mobile phone - Relevant chat groups and call records

    This evidence can help investigators reconstruct what happened and establish connections between the suspect and the crime.


    Important Part: Evidence Handling
    Digital forensics isn't simply:
    "Find files → read them → report them."

    Investigators must follow proper procedures for:

    Collect
    ↓
    Preserve
    ↓
    Store
    ↓
    Analyze
    ↓
    Document
    ↓
    Report

    This is important because digital evidence can be easily changed, deleted, or damaged. Proper handling helps maintain its integrity and reliability so it can be used during legal proceedings.

**Key takeaway:** 

    Digital forensics involves properly collecting, preserving, analyzing, and reporting digital evidence so investigators can understand a crime and support legal proceedings.
