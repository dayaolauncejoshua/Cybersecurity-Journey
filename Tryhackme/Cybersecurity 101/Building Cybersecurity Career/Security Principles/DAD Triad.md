# DAD Triad

    Represents three types of attacks that are the opposite of the CIA Triad.

### 1.Disclosure - Opposite of Confidentiality

    Confidential information is exposed to unauthorized people.

    Example: An attacker steals patient medical records and publishes them online.

### 2.Alteration - Opposite of Integrity

    Information is changed without authorization, potentially making it inaccurate or misleading.

    Example: An attacker modifies a patient's medical record, causing a doctor to administer the wrong treatment.

### 3.Destruction/Denial - Opposite of Availability

    Data is destroyed or access to a system or service is prevented.

    Example: An attacker makes a hospital's patient database unavailable, preventing staff from accessing medical records.

### Balancing the CIA Triad

    Security principles must be balanced because strengthening one can sometimes make another harder to maintain.

    Examples: 

    Excessively restrictive access controls may protect confidentiality but prevent authorized employees from accessing information when needed.

    Making data widely accessible may improve availability but increase the risk of unauthorized disclosure or modification.

    Strict integrity checks may add processing time, potentially affecting system availability.

    The goal is not to maximize one principle at all costs. It is to protect confidentiality, integrity, and availability according to the system's risks and requirements.

### Key Takes

    CIA describes what we want to protect, while DAD describes what attackers may do to undermine it.
