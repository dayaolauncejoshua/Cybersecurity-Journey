# CIA The Security Triad: Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability

    The CIA Triad is a fundamental information security model consisting of Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability. These principles help protect data and systems.

### 1.Confidentiality

    Ensures only authorized people can access sensitive data or systems.

    Examples:

    Online Shopping: Credit card information should only be accessible to authorized parties.
    Healthcare: Patient records must be protected from unauthorized disclosure.
    
    Access controls, encryption, and permissions help protect confidentiality

### 2.Integrity

    Aims to ensure data is not altered or changed and detect alteration if it occurs.

    Examples:
    Online shopping: An attacker must not be able to change the shipping address to their own.
    Healthcare: A patient's medical record must not be changed incorrectly, potentially causing the wrong treatment.

    Hashes can help detect file changes: access controls help prevent unauthorized modifications.

### 3.Availability

    Aims to ensure that systems, services, data are available to authorized users when needed.

    Examples:
    Online shopping: The website must be working so customers can place orders.
    Healthcare: Doctors need access to patient records when providing treatment.

    Backups, redundant systems, and DDoS protection help maintain availability.

### Beyond the CIA Triad

    Authenticity

    Ensures that data, files, messages, or orders are genuinely from the source they claim to come from.

    Example: A company receives an order for 1000 cars. It needs to verift that the order genuinely came from an authorized customer, rather than a fraduster pretending to be that customer.

    Digital signatures and authentication mechanisms can help verify the source of information.


    Nonrepudiation

    Provides evidence that makes it difficult for someone to deny an action or transaction they performed.

    Example: A company needs reliable evidence that a customer placed a large order, so the customer cannot simply deny having submitted it.

    Cybersecurity relevance: Digital signatures and appropriately maintained audit logs can help provide evidence of who performed an action.

### Parkerian Hexad

    The Parkerian Hexad, proposed by Donn Parker in 1998, expands the CIA Triad into six security elements.

    1. Confidentiality

    2. Integrity

    3. Availability

    4. Authenticity - Ensures something is genine or source verification

    5. Utility - Ensures information remains useful in its available form.

    6. Possession - Concerns who physically or logically controls the information
